# Fractions

## Numerator and denominator

In a fraction the denominator (bottom number) says how many equal parts the whole is cut into.
The numerator (top number) says how many of those parts you have. In 2/7, the whole is cut
into 7 parts and you have 2 of them.

## Adding and subtracting fractions

You can only add or subtract fractions with the same denominator. Find a common denominator
first, rewrite each fraction, then add or subtract the numerators. The denominator stays the
same. Example: 1/3 + 1/9 = 3/9 + 1/9 = 4/9.

## Multiplying and dividing fractions

To multiply, multiply the numerators together and the denominators together. You can cancel a
common factor first to keep the numbers small. To divide by a fraction, multiply by its
reciprocal (flip it upside down). Example: 2/9 ÷ 1/3 = 2/9 × 3 = 6/9.

## Simplest form and percentages

A fraction is in simplest form when the numerator and denominator have no common factor except
1. Divide both by their highest common factor. Per cent means "out of 100", so 40% = 40/100.
A fraction "of" a number means multiply: 3/10 of 50 is 50 ÷ 10 × 3.
