# English grammar

## Past simple of irregular verbs

Regular verbs add -ed in the past simple (play, played). Irregular verbs change their form and
must be learned: see / saw, take / took, eat / ate, think / thought, bring / brought.

## Present perfect and past participles

The present perfect is have/has + past participle: "I have finished". For irregular verbs the
participle is often different from the past simple: take / took / taken, eat / ate / eaten.

## Present simple with he, she, it

With he, she or it, the present simple verb adds -s: she plays. Verbs ending in -ch, -sh, -s,
-x or -o add -es: he fixes, she goes. A consonant + y changes to -ies: it flies.

## Comparatives and superlatives

Short adjectives add -er and -est: tall, taller, the tallest. A final consonant + y becomes
-ier / -iest: happy, happier. One vowel + one consonant doubles the consonant: hot, hotter,
the hottest. Long adjectives use more and most: more careful, the most careful.

## Irregular plurals

Most nouns add -s, but some plurals are irregular: man / men, woman / women, foot / feet,
mouse / mice, person / people.
